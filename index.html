What this is

Meme coins end. All of them. There is no drain at the bottom of the pool.

ANTI is the drain. Not a competitor. The destination.

No product. No roadmap. No promises.

What that means

The founder does not set supply and does not set liquidity. No tokens are allocated to anyone — not to a team, not to investors, not to advisors, not to marketing, not to the founder.

The founder can buy, the same way anyone else can. A purchase is not an allocation. That difference is the whole point.

That is not restraint. Launching this way means an allocation is not among the options.

That is a better guarantee than principle.

The Ritual Burn

A meme coin dies. It gets buried.

The project acquires a quantity of the dead coin and sends it to a burn address. The transaction is public. The coin receives a permanent listing in the Meme Graveyard, with its burn hash.

Dead coins are cheap. That is the entry requirement. One ceremony costs a few dollars for the coin, plus gas.

The quantity burned will often be enormous. This is not a flex. It is a consequence of the coin being worthless.

There is no limit on the number of ceremonies. A cemetery that stops accepting bodies is a strange cemetery.

What counts as dead

Three conditions. All required.

  • Collapsed — trading at 1% or less of its all-time high.
  • Abandoned — three or fewer trades a day, every day, for thirty days.
  • Buyable — at least one pool with something left in it.

That is the whole test. There is no assessment of whether a coin was a scam, because that asks what people meant to do. These ask whether anyone is still trading it.

A coin with nothing left to buy cannot be buried. Those are not listed. The graveyard can only bury what it can reach.

Chain is not a condition. ANTI launches on Chewpad; it buries from anywhere. A meme coin that died on another chain is still a dead meme coin.

The graveyard does not care how you died. No meme is immune from burial.

How a coin is chosen

The graveyard does not choose. It ranks.

Every meme coin CoinGecko has ever catalogued is checked, every day. Those meeting all three conditions are ordered by how far they fell, largest first. Whatever sits at the front is next.

The register is not ours. Being catalogued as a meme coin is a judgement someone else made, years before ANTI existed, with nothing at stake in the outcome. We do not decide what counts as a meme, and we do not keep a list of our own.

Nothing is hand-picked. There is no nomination and no vote. The ranking is arithmetic on public data, and anyone can recompute it.

There is no vote. A number you can check yourself is not a thing worth voting on.

Before any burial

Every burial is announced before it happens, with the evidence — what it peaked at, what it trades at now, how many trades a day, how long it has been watched, and where to check each.

Then 72 hours. If the finding is wrong, say so inside that window.

Candidates that fail are published too, with the reason. A process that only ever produces burials is a rubber stamp.

How the burn is paid for

Not with ANTI.

There is no ANTI-denominated burn fund, and none is needed. Nothing in the ceremony requires ANTI to be sold, so no ANTI is sold.

Ceremonies are funded in SOL from a public founder wallet. The address is published. The balance is visible. It goes down.

When it is empty, ceremonies stop until it is refilled. You will see that before we say it.

Holders do not fund the graveyard. The founder does.

What the founder holds

Nothing at launch. The founder takes no position in the sale.

Any position is bought afterward, on the open market, at whatever it costs by then — the same way anyone else would have to. It will be disclosed with its address and transaction hash.

There is no allocation to disclose, because there is no allocation. And there is no launch position to disclose, because there is no launch position.
